    Edge length that varies: a sizing mesh¶

    The meshing and remeshing components aim for one edge length everywhere: length_rel times the input's bounding box diagonal, or length in the input's units. Only where the quality gets stuck do they refine on their own. Often a simulation wants something else: small elements near a crack, a load or a thin part, large ones far from it.

    A sizing mesh gives the largest edge length as a field over space. TriWild, TetWild, SimWild and isotropic remeshing take one as their sizing_mesh option:

    Component The sizing mesh Where a point reads it
    TriWild triangles, in the plane the triangle containing it
    TetWild tetrahedra the tet containing it
    SimWild triangles in 2D, tetrahedra in 3D the cell containing it
    Isotropic remeshing a triangle surface the point of the surface closest to it

    The option is optional: without it, every component runs exactly as before.

    The sizing mesh¶

    Any mesh with those cells whose vertices carry the length, in the input's units, as a vertex field named target_length: one positive value per vertex, interpolated linearly inside the cells. It does not have to match the input: a few large cells around it are enough for a smooth field. In Python it is a mesh dictionary, here a square of two triangles whose length grows from 1% of a circle's size on its left side to 6% on its right:

    # A square around the input, two triangles, whose length grows from `left` on its left side to
# `right` on its right: interpolated linearly in between.
def planar_sizing(lo, hi, left, right):
    V = np.array([[lo, lo, 0], [hi, lo, 0], [hi, hi, 0], [lo, hi, 0]], dtype=float)
    F = np.array([[0, 1, 2], [0, 2, 3]])
    return {
        "V": V,
        "cells": {"triangle": {"connectivity": F}},
        "vertex_attributes": {"target_length": np.array([left, right, right, left])},
    }


# The lengths are in the input's units: from 1% of the circle's size to 6%. The square reaches a
# fifth of that size past the circle on each side, beyond the background mesh TriWild builds
# around its input: a point outside every triangle takes the global length.
circle = wm.read_mesh(str(DATA / "triwild_circle.obj"))
lo, hi = circle["V"][:, :2].min(), circle["V"][:, :2].max()
size = hi - lo
lo, hi = lo - 0.2 * size, hi + 0.2 * size
sizing = planar_sizing(lo, hi, 0.01 * size, 0.06 * size)

    A point outside every cell is not covered, and takes the component's global length there. Make the sizing mesh cover more than the input: TriWild and TetWild mesh a box larger than the input's bounding box (by 1/15 of its diagonal on each side), and its vertices are sized too. A surface sizing mesh covers every point: isotropic remeshing reads it at the closest point.

    TriWild¶

    On the memory route, sizing_mesh names a mesh of the call. The inside of the circle, first at the default length_rel 0.05, then with the sizing mesh. Each edge is compared with the field at its midpoint:

    # The inside of the circle, with and without the sizing mesh, sequentially (num_threads 0) so
# that the numbers repeat exactly.
options = {"input": ["circle"], "filter": "input", "stop_energy": 10, "num_threads": 0}
for name, extra in [("length_rel 0.05", {}), ("sizing mesh", {"sizing_mesh": "sizing"})]:
    outputs, report = wm.triwild({**options, **extra}, {"circle": circle, "sizing": sizing})
    triangles = len(outputs["mesh"]["cells"]["triangle"]["connectivity"])
    print(f"triwild, {name}: {triangles} triangles after {report['#iterations']} iteration(s), "
          f"{against(outputs['mesh'], 'triangle', field)}")

    wmtk::MeshData circle = wmtk::io::read_mesh(data / "triwild_circle.obj");
const double size = circle.V.leftCols(2).maxCoeff() - circle.V.leftCols(2).minCoeff();
const double lo = circle.V.leftCols(2).minCoeff() - 0.2 * size;
const double hi = circle.V.leftCols(2).maxCoeff() + 0.2 * size;

// A square around the circle, past the background mesh TriWild builds around it, two
// triangles, whose vertex field target_length grows from 1% of the circle's size on its left
// side to 6% on its right, in the input's units.
wmtk::MeshData sizing;
sizing.V.resize(4, 3);
sizing.V << lo, lo, 0, hi, lo, 0, hi, hi, 0, lo, hi, 0;
sizing.cells[wmtk::CellType::Triangle].connectivity.resize(2, 3);
sizing.cells[wmtk::CellType::Triangle].connectivity << 0, 1, 2, 0, 2, 3;
wmtk::DataMatrix<double> length(4, 1);
length << 0.01 * size, 0.06 * size, 0.06 * size, 0.01 * size;
sizing.vertex_attributes["target_length"].values = length;

wmtk::MeshMap meshes{{"circle", circle}, {"sizing", sizing}};
auto [outputs, report] = wmtk::components::triwild::triwild(
    {{"input", nlohmann::json::array({"circle"})},
     {"sizing_mesh", "sizing"},
     {"filter", "input"},
     {"stop_energy", 10},
     {"num_threads", 0}},
    meshes);

// Every edge against the field at its midpoint: the share longer than 4/3 of it, those a
// split would cut. The field is a largest length; shorter is fine.
const wmtk::MeshData& mesh = outputs.at("mesh");
const auto& F = mesh.cell(wmtk::CellType::Triangle).connectivity;
size_t total = 0, too_long = 0;
for (Eigen::Index f = 0; f < F.rows(); ++f) {
    for (int k = 0; k < 3; ++k) {
        const Eigen::RowVector3d a = mesh.V.row(F(f, k)), b = mesh.V.row(F(f, (k + 1) % 3));
        const double t = (0.5 * (a.x() + b.x()) - lo) / (hi - lo);
        const double field = (0.01 + 0.05 * t) * size;
        ++total; // each interior edge twice, as both its triangles see it
        if ((a - b).norm() > 4. / 3. * field) ++too_long;
    }
}
std::cout << F.rows() << " triangles, " << 100. * too_long / total
          << "% of the edges past 4/3 of the field\n";
wmtk::io::write_mesh("circle.msh", mesh);

    triwild, length_rel 0.05: 289 triangles after 1 iteration(s), 52.9% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 1.38 of it
triwild, sizing mesh: 840 triangles after 1 iteration(s), 0.3% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 0.84 of it

    On the file route, sizing_mesh is the path of a mesh file, relative to input_dir like the inputs. A .msh file written by wm.write_mesh keeps the field, as a node field target_length:

    # The same run on files: the sizing mesh is a mesh file whose vertices carry the field
# target_length, relative to input_dir like the inputs.
wm.write_mesh("sizing.msh", sizing)
wm.triwild({**options, "input": [str(DATA / "triwild_circle.obj")], "sizing_mesh": "sizing.msh",
            "output": "circle"})
print(f"triwild on files: {against(wm.read_mesh('circle.msh'), 'triangle', field)}")

    triwild on files: 0.3% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 0.84 of it

    On the command line, the same is "sizing_mesh": "sizing.msh" in the JSON.

    A largest length, not a target¶

    In TriWild, TetWild and SimWild every vertex carries a sizing, the factor of the target length its edges are split and collapsed to (an edge longer than 4/3 of its target is split, one shorter than 4/5 collapsed; the optimization loop). The sizing mesh sets the sizing of every vertex it covers when the optimization starts, to the field's length over the global one, and caps it after every pass:

    • New vertices follow the field. A split's new vertex takes the mean of its ends' sizing, and the cap lowers it where the field asks for less.
    • Finer is allowed. Where the quality gets stuck, the stuck refinement lowers the sizing further, and the cap only ever lowers it. A collapse that would make an element worse is still refused. Either way the mesh ends finer than the field there, and that is a valid result: the median edge above is 0.84 of the field.
    • Longer than length is allowed. The field may ask for more than the global length; length and length_rel then only apply where it does not reach.

    Keeping the edges under the field¶

    Two things hold the edges under the field, both only with a sizing mesh:

    • limit_collapse_length is on unless set to false, in all four components. A collapse is judged by the edge it removes; without the limit, it may leave edges longer than their target, and since the optimization ends on collapses, nothing splits them again. With it, no collapse leaves an edge longer than 4/3 of its target, the length above which a split would cut it, as Botsch and Kobbelt's isotropic remeshing does.
    • The optimization does not stop on quality alone (TriWild, TetWild, SimWild). It goes on past stop_energy while more than 1% of the edges are longer than 4/3 of their target and that share still drops by a tenth from one iteration to the next, up to max_iterations. Edges shorter than the field never keep it going.

    Turned off, the limit costs fewer elements and more edges past the field; the second rule then runs one more iteration:

    # With a sizing mesh, limit_collapse_length is on unless set: no collapse leaves an edge longer
# than 4/3 of its target. Turned off, collapses merge edges past the field:
for limit in [True, False]:
    outputs, report = wm.triwild(
        {**options, "sizing_mesh": "sizing", "limit_collapse_length": limit},
        {"circle": circle, "sizing": sizing})
    triangles = len(outputs["mesh"]["cells"]["triangle"]["connectivity"])
    print(f"limit_collapse_length {limit}: {triangles} triangles after {report['#iterations']} "
          f"iteration(s), {against(outputs['mesh'], 'triangle', field)}")

    limit_collapse_length True: 840 triangles after 1 iteration(s), 0.3% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 0.84 of it
limit_collapse_length False: 577 triangles after 2 iteration(s), 13.0% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 1.02 of it

    Smoothing can still stretch an edge a little past 4/3 of its target: to keep every edge under a bound, give about 3/4 of it as the field.

    The field is read at the vertices: an edge's target is the mean of its ends'. A field smooth at the scale of the edges, as above, is kept closely; one that varies a lot within an edge's length is followed less well, since the field between the ends does not count: give the sizing mesh cells no smaller than the edges you expect. The stuck refinement grades its own refinement into the neighbourhood, but not past a vertex whose sizing the field set above the global length.

    TetWild¶

    The sizing mesh is a tet mesh. A box around a sphere of radius 0.7, as five tets, whose length grows from 0.03 at its bottom to 0.15 at its top:

    # TetWild: the sizing mesh is a tet mesh. A box around a sphere of radius 0.7, as five tets,
# whose length grows from 0.03 at its bottom to 0.15 at its top: linear in z.
def box_sizing(lo, hi, bottom, top):
    x0, y0, z0 = lo
    x1, y1, z1 = hi
    V = np.array([[x, y, z] for z in (z0, z1) for y in (y0, y1) for x in (x0, x1)], dtype=float)
    T = np.array([[0, 1, 3, 5], [0, 3, 2, 6], [0, 5, 4, 6], [3, 5, 6, 7], [0, 3, 6, 5]])
    return {"V": V, "cells": {"tetrahedron": {"connectivity": T}},
            "vertex_attributes": {"target_length": np.where(V[:, 2] == z0, bottom, top)}}


sphere = wm.read_mesh(str(DATA / "sphere.obj"))
margin = 0.2 * np.linalg.norm(sphere["V"].max(axis=0) - sphere["V"].min(axis=0))
box_lo, box_hi = sphere["V"].min(axis=0) - margin, sphere["V"].max(axis=0) + margin
box = box_sizing(box_lo, box_hi, 0.03, 0.15)
for name, extra in [("length_rel 0.05", {}), ("sizing mesh", {"sizing_mesh": "box"})]:
    outputs, report = wm.tetwild(
        {"input": ["sphere"], "filter": "tracked", "num_threads": 0, **extra},
        {"sphere": sphere, "box": box})
    tets = len(outputs["mesh"]["cells"]["tetrahedron"]["connectivity"])
    print(f"tetwild, {name}: {tets} tets, " + against(
        outputs["mesh"], "tetrahedron", lambda p: np.interp(p[:, 2], [box_lo[2], box_hi[2]],
                                                            [0.03, 0.15])))

    tetwild, length_rel 0.05: 12687 tets, 29.6% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 1.16 of it
tetwild, sizing mesh: 34011 tets, 0.6% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 0.85 of it

    Isotropic remeshing¶

    The sizing mesh is a surface, and isotropic remeshing reads it at the point closest to each vertex, again wherever an operation moves one; an edge's target is the mean of its ends'. For isotropic remeshing the field is a target rather than a largest length, as length is: it splits and collapses every edge toward it, for a fixed number of iterations, with no stuck refinement and no energy target to stop at. The input itself can be the sizing mesh: here the bunny, with a field that goes from 4% of its diagonal at its feet to 1% at its ears:

    # Isotropic remeshing: the sizing mesh is a surface, read at the point closest to each vertex.
# Here it is the input itself, with a field that goes from 4% of the bunny's diagonal at its
# feet to 1% at its ears: linear in y.
bunny = wm.read_mesh(str(DATA / "bunny.obj"))
y0, y1 = bunny["V"][:, 1].min(), bunny["V"][:, 1].max()
diag = np.linalg.norm(bunny["V"].max(axis=0) - bunny["V"].min(axis=0))
height = (bunny["V"][:, 1] - y0) / (y1 - y0)
bunny_sizing = dict(bunny, vertex_attributes={"target_length": diag * (0.04 - 0.03 * height)})
for name, extra in [("length_rel 0.025", {"length_rel": 0.025}),
                    ("sizing mesh", {"sizing_mesh": "field"})]:
    outputs, _ = wm.isotropic_remeshing({"input": "bunny", "max_iterations": 5, **extra},
                                        {"bunny": bunny, "field": bunny_sizing})
    triangles = len(outputs["mesh"]["cells"]["triangle"]["connectivity"])
    print(f"isotropic_remeshing, {name}: {triangles} triangles, " + against(
        outputs["mesh"], "triangle", lambda p: diag * np.interp(p[:, 1], [y0, y1], [0.04, 0.01])))

    isotropic_remeshing, length_rel 0.025: 2559 triangles, 21.0% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 0.96 of it
isotropic_remeshing, sizing mesh: 3633 triangles, 0.5% of the edges past 4/3 of the field, median 1.02 of it

    SimWild: with regions¶

    SimWild also sizes by region: sizing_field gives a length per region expression. With both, a vertex takes the smaller of its regions' length and the sizing mesh's; where only the sizing mesh reaches, its length, even longer than length; where only a region, the region's. Only the remeshing operation optimizes: the region operations ignore both options, so one set of options can be passed to every step of a pipeline.

    The coarsening pass¶

    The optional coarsening pass of TriWild, TetWild and SimWild removes the elements the quality target does not need. Left unbounded (the default), it ignores every target length, the field's too. Bounded, it leaves the edges at or past 4/5 of the target: with a sizing mesh, the field's length at the edge's ends, also where that is longer than length.

    Summary¶

    For TriWild, TetWild and SimWild; isotropic remeshing shares the first and the limit_collapse_length rows.

    Without a sizing mesh With one
    Largest edge length length, or length_rel times the diagonal the field's where it covers the vertex, else as without
    Finer than that stuck refinement, and collapses refused on quality the same
    limit_collapse_length off unless set on unless set to false
    Stops when the energy is under stop_energy the same, and at most 1% of the edges past 4/3 of their target, or no more progress
    Bounded coarsening stops at 4/5 of length stops at 4/5 of the field's length
    SimWild's sizing_field the region's length the smaller of the region's and the field's
