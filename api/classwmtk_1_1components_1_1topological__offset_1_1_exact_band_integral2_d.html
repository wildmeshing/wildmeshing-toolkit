Detailed Description

The band-area integral of one triangle, in closed form – the 2D twin of ExactBandIntegral (2026-11-10, the 2D lock-step port): 

J(t) = int_t (d(x) - delta) dA,   d = Euclidean distance to the input,

the input a complex of segments and points in the plane (a 2D offset's input complex: the boundary of a tagged region, open curves, isolated points). The algorithm, its constants and its tolerances are ExactBandIntegral's one dimension down; this doc states where they differ.

Features and local regions. The features are the segments' interiors (edges) and the vertices (segment ends and points, merged by exact coordinates and sorted, as 3D's). An edge's region is its slab between the lines through its ends perpendicular to it; a vertex's region is the cone {x : (x - v).(w - v) <= 0 for every edge (v, w)}, all of the plane for an isolated point. d = min over {g : x in L_g} of d_g is exact, d_g the line or point distance of g, by 3D's two facts. Where 2D differs: an edge's line distance |n.(x - a)| is affine on each side of its line, so a 2D edge takes the role of a 3D face in every integral and split (3D's line distance has no 2D counterpart), and the slab is not cut by incident faces (a segment has none).

Per triangle, in its own frame (origin the centroid, as 3D's cell; the same reason): 0. Candidates: U = min over the primitives P (segments, points) of max over t's corners of d_P bounds d on t (d_P convex); every feature whose box is within U of t's box is kept.

  1. t is cut by the candidates' regions into convex pieces of constant member set; members that cannot attain the minimum (lower bound >= another member's upper bound) are dropped.
  2. Exact: one member; all members edges, split by their lines and then by the bisectors of their (then affine) distances, as 3D splits a piece whose members are all faces – two lines in the plane meet or are parallel, so every edge pair is exact here, unlike 3D's skew edges; all members vertices, split by their perpendicular bisectors.
  3. Otherwise – an edge and a vertex not its own, whose bisector is a parabola – "rule (a)": the piece takes min over its members g of int_piece d_g >= int_piece d, an upper bound, as 3D's curved bisectors do. Stats::rule_a_area reports its area. The pieces must tile t and leave no more of it outside every region than the partition's tolerances allow; value() throws otherwise, with t in hex floats (3D's check_cell_pieces()).

One-feature integrals: an affine distance is area times its centroid value; a point distance by Euler's theorem for degree-1 homogeneous integrands, int_Q |x - v| dA = 1/3 sum over Q's edges e of h_e int_e |x - v| ds, h_e the signed distance from v to e's line, the edge integral in 3D's rationalized form (int_sqrt()). 3D's conditioning remark holds: a piece of size L at distance D from its vertex loses about eps (D/L)^2 in the Euler sum.

The offset side, as 3D's: constructed with per-segment sides (+1 if the segment's normal (b - a) rotated clockwise points out of the solid the segments bound, -1 into it, 0 no solid) and sigma (+1: the band is outside the solid, -1 inside, 0: no clip), value() integrates only the offset side. The side of x is decided by its nearest feature g, by the sign of (x - q_g).N_g: N_g the segment's outward normal (edge), the sum of its two segments' unit outward normals (vertex, the plane's pseudonormal; 3D's angle weights are equal in the plane). A vertex has a side only with exactly two segments, both sided, as a 3D edge only with exactly two faces. Where 2D differs: 3D's segments bound no solid and never have a side; a 2D segment is a 3D triangle's twin here. The clip rules are 3D's (class doc there): a one-member part is clipped by its member's line, an edges-only part kept or dropped whole, a rule-(a) piece split by its members' lines with a disagreeing part taking the side of the member nearest at its centroid. With nothing clipped J = int_t d - delta area(t), as 3D keeps.

value() sums the elementary pieces (pieces on which the integrand is one smooth function) that value_grad_hess() differentiates, so the two agree bit for bit. Where 2D differs: 3D's evaluate() integrates the pieces directly and captures the elementary pieces into a Partition for the derivative call that follows (a speed device MEASURED on 3D cells); one build serves both here until a 2D measurement asks for the capture.

Thread safety: every member is const and keeps no state between calls.

The band integral's dependence through one boundary edge of the band, for the motion of its corner kv (the other fixed): the 2D twin of ExactBandIntegral:: front_face_derivs() under a CurvedBisectorRuleA, EXPERIMENTAL_band_volume_rule "exact_swept"'s search model (BandVolumeEnergy2D::set_swept()). f = (f0, f1) ordered so that its normal n = rot_cw(f1 - f0) / |.| points out of the band, x = f[kv]: grad = n int_f chi (d - delta) phi ds, hess = n (x) int_f chi phi^2 grad d ds + (int_f chi (d - delta) phi ds / |N|) dN/dx.

  • sum over cut points c: n (x) g_c (d - delta)(c) phi(c)^2 / |g_c,f|, hess_swept = (n (x) Q + Q (x) n) / 2, Q = int_f chi phi^2 grad d ds - sum over c: g_c (d - delta)(c) phi(c)^2 / |g_c,f|, phi the hat function of x along f, chi the offset side's indicator, N = rot_cw(f1 - f0), the cut points the ends of f's parts that are not ends of f, each with its part's own integrand (g_c the normal of the line that cut f there, out of the part, g_c,f its part along f). As in 3D: summed over the band's boundary edges through x, grad and hess are the band integral's gradient and Hessian in x (Reynolds' transport theorem, no medial-axis term, d being Lipschitz); hess_swept is the Hessian at w = 0 of the integral over the swept triangle conv(x, x + w, f[1 - kv]) signed by w.n, its labels f's; a part of f on the input takes grad d from the band side (-n) unless it is the offset side of a sided segment.

f's parts, labelled as value() labels the pieces of a thin swept triangle over f (3D's label_face()): f cut by the candidates' regions into spans with member sets (max_distance()'s partition: inside first where f lies on a region's line, as 3D's face partition), the members pruned on the span's ends, then one member, all edges (split by their lines, then by their bisectors), all vertices (split by their perpendicular bisectors), or rule (a): the span split by the members' side lines, each part labelled by the member of smallest int_part d_g phi ds (the first on a tie), a part where the lines disagree taking the side of the member nearest at its centroid in that limit (below). So summed over the boundary edges, grad at x is the exact derivative at x of the band change "exact_swept" judges a move from x on, where value() and these labels cut alike. rule_a: the number of parts labelled by rule (a).

Where 2D differs, and why:

  • a curved bisector never throws: the parts are always labelled by rule (a), 3D's behaviour under a CurvedBisectorRuleA, which "exact_swept"'s search model always opens; 3D's throwing mode serves "front_flux", which has no 2D port. So no CurvedBisectorRuleA: the corner whose hat function weighs rule (a)'s integrals is kv;
  • the centroid that decides a part whose members' side lines disagree is the thin swept triangle's in the limit, the phi-weighted centroid of the span, where 3D's label_face() takes the face part's own area centroid: value() decides by the swept part's centroid, which lies over the phi-weighted one;
  • no labelled parts are kept between calls (3D's FaceParts) and there is no front_face(): the 2D integral keeps no partitions (class doc) and has no front form;
  • a span lying on a bisector line takes its lower member, as value() breaks a tie between parallel lines (3D's resolve() keeps both sides of a ring on a bisector plane). Throws (f in hex floats) if the parts do not tile f, or leave more of f outside every region, than the tolerances allow (3D's check_face_pieces(), on lengths).

max over the segment f of d: 3D's max_distance() over a front triangle one dimension down, the settle test's outside_f under EXPERIMENTAL_settle_mark "max". f is cut by the candidates' local regions as a triangle is (the same search, region tests, tolerances and scale rule; a segment in place of 3D's planar ring), and d is convex wherever one member's distance is d, so on each piece the max is at an end of the piece:

  • one member: the max of d_g at the piece's two ends, exact;
  • members all edges, or all vertices: split by the members' lines and then by the bisectors of their (then affine) distances, or by the vertices' perpendicular bisectors, each sub-piece one affine or point distance, exact (3D's all-faces and all-vertices cases);
  • otherwise (rule (a): an edge and a vertex not its own, whose bisector is a parabola): min over members g of the max of d_g at the piece's ends, an upper bound (d <= d_g). The max over the pieces is exact up to rounding unless it comes from a rule-(a) piece; never below the true max. As in 3D, a segment lying ON a region's boundary line counts as inside that region (a point of the line is in both closed regions): a duplicated piece cannot lower a max, a lost one can.

Throws (with f in hex floats) if the pieces do not tile f, or leave more of f outside every region, than the tolerances allow (3D's checks on areas, here on lengths).

J and its gradient and Hessian with respect to corner kv of t, the other two fixed: 3D's value_grad_hess() one dimension down. Reynolds' transport theorem on each elementary piece Q, a boundary integral over the two edges of t through x = t[kv] (outward unit normal n, length |N|, lambda the hat function of x): grad += n int_{Q cap e} lambda phi ds, hess += n (x) int_{Q cap e} lambda^2 grad phi ds + (int_{Q cap e} lambda phi ds / |N|) dN/dx.

  • sum over the ends y of Q cap e that are not corners of t: n (x) g lambda(y)^2 phi(y) / |g - (g.n) n|, phi the piece's integrand, g the outward normal of Q's edge through y ("cut points", 3D's cut edges: they cancel in pairs where phi is continuous and leave its jump where it is not). A piece touching e at one vertex only adds that vertex's two cut terms (3D's contact term). Where the derivative is not the derivative of J, as in 3D: on rule-(a) pieces it is that of the bound (the chosen member's integral); with a kink of d on an edge of t through x, the one-sided limit for x moving into t. Flat triangles give J = 0 and zero derivatives. Layout and orientation as 3D's: one corner at a time, grad its 2-vector, hess its 2 x 2 block; t in either orientation.
