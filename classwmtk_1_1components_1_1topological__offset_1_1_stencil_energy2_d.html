Detailed Description

THE offset term of a 2D front vertex's smoothing objective: the mean squared relative error of the field over the stencil of each incident front chord. 

E(x) = w * sum over the vertex's incident front chords e of
           (1/N_s) * sum over e's N_s stencil points i of  r(q_i(x))^2

r(p) = (Phi(p) - c) / c,      q_i(x) = a_i x + b_i q1

with (a_i, b_i) the barycentric weights of stencil point i on the chord, a_i the MOVING vertex's own weight, and q1 the chord's other end, fixed for the visit. The stencil is TopoOffsetTriMesh::for_each_edge_sample, sized by stencil_order.

UNITS: the front smoother passes w = 1 / front_conv_frac()^2 (TopoOffsetTriMesh::offset_term_weight()), which puts r in units of the tolerance: each chord's term is then TopoOffsetTriMesh::edge_offset_term(), 1 at the bar, the term the per-cell energy tri_energy() adds to the chord's band face, and E is the sum of those terms over the vertex's chords. Exactly so for the euclidean field, where r IS relative_residual().

THIS ONE TERM REPLACES BOTH the placement term (OffsetEnergy2D on the vertex alone) and the sag term over the chord interiors that preceded it, because the stencil contains the ends: at order 0 the stencil IS the two ends, so E is exactly the placement residual of the vertex and its neighbours, and every higher order adds interior points that ask the same question between them.

r IS THE PLAIN RELATIVE ERROR (Phi - c)/c, which for the euclidean field is exactly (d - target_distance)/target_distance – the same residual OffsetEnergy2D uses there. For the SMOOTH field OffsetEnergy2D instead divides by g_ref * delta to get a monotone length; this class does not, so under offset_field: "smooth" the two are scaled differently (about 3.44x the criterion's number at the default offset_dhat_factor).

NOTE THE PER-CHORD MEAN, SUMMED OVER CHORDS, with no length weight: a vertex with V incident chords contributes its own r(x)^2 with coefficient V/N_s, since it is a stencil point of every one of them.

The derivatives are exact, and so is the Hessian by default: per stencil point 2 a_i^2 (dr dr^T + r hess Phi / c), whose second term is indefinite where r < 0 (inside the level set). gauss_newton drops that term, leaving the sum of a_i^2 dr dr^T outer products, PSD by construction – the form used until 2026-09-28; hessian() says why the default changed.
